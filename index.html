Adaptive Wireless Power Transfer

Mapping coil coupling from transmitter current, then tuning (C1, f) around the coupled modes

In one minute

When an EV charging pad is misaligned, the mutual inductance M between the coils changes, and a fixed-tuned charger either loses power or exceeds its current limits. We simulate a 3.3 kW, 85 kHz series–series link and adapt two transmitter-side knobs: a switched capacitor bank C1 and the inverter frequency f. The receiver is unmodified and M is never measured directly.

Actuators move the receiver through 1,485 poses. Three low-power current readings per pose recover |M| to about 2%. We then solve for the two coupled-mode resonances analytically and probe only narrow windows around them, which gives the same power as an exhaustive sweep with about 13× fewer probes.

How it works

  1. Source: 230 V mains → rectifier → DC link → inverter at 79–90 kHz.
  2. Map M(space): actuators step x, y, gap and tilt. At each pose, |M| is fitted from the measured I1 phasor.
  3. Predict the modes: the mapped M goes into the resonance quadratic, giving two candidate frequencies per C1 state.
  4. Probe narrowly: sweep ±3 kHz around each mode, with a fallback near the receiver resonance.
  5. Select and verify: take the best safe (C1, f) within the I1, VC1, η and ZVS limits.

Governing physics

At 85 kHz the wavelength is about 3.5 km, much larger than the 0.5 m pad, so the near field is magnetoquasistatic.
